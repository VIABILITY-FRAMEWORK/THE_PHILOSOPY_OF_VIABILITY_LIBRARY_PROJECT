
\begin{table}[htbp]
    \centering
    \caption{Conceptual Distinctions Concerning Ontological Identity}
    \label{tab:identity-conceptual-distinctions}

    \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.35}
    \begin{tabular}{p{3.2cm} p{5.2cm} p{5.2cm}}
        \hline
        \textbf{Concept} &
        \textbf{Ontological Meaning} &
        \textbf{Distinction from Identity} \\
        \hline

        Being &
        The actuality of an entity as something that is. Being is not an indeterminate substrate to which identity is subsequently added. &
        Being and identity are ontologically inseparable, although they remain analytically distinguishable. \\

        Identity &
        The constitutive determination through which an entity is the entity that it is. &
        Identity is not an additional property, external description, or secondary attribute of an already constituted entity. \\

        Unity &
        The coherence through which an entity constitutes a unified ontological whole. &
        Unity concerns the coherence of an entity and does not, by itself, establish its numerical identity or uniqueness. \\

        Singularity &
        The condition of being one rather than many within a specified determination or domain. &
        Singularity is not equivalent to identity. An entity's identity cannot be reduced to its numerical oneness. \\

        Individuation &
        The ontological determination through which an entity is distinguishable as this entity rather than another. &
        Individuation concerns distinction and delimitation, whereas identity concerns what constitutes the entity as itself. \\

        Determination &
        The establishment of a definite ontological character or condition. &
        Determination contributes to the constitution of identity but should not be treated as synonymous with identity. \\

        Difference &
        The ontological distinction between entities, determinations, or conditions. &
        Difference makes distinction possible but does not, by itself, provide a complete account of the identity of what differs. \\

        Invariance &
        The persistence of a specified determination across a defined range of transformations or variations. &
        Invariance may be relevant to the preservation or analysis of identity, but identity cannot be defined in advance as invariance alone. \\

        Signature &
        A set or configuration of features through which an entity may be characterized or distinguished. &
        A signature may express or indicate aspects of identity, but it is not identical to the identity it characterizes. \\

        Expression &
        The manner in which an entity's determinations become expressed in a particular form or configuration. &
        Expression concerns the articulation of identity and must not be assumed to exhaust or constitute identity in its entirety. \\

        Manifestation &
        The ontological realization of an entity or determination within a particular domain or receiving context. &
        Manifestation is a mode or condition of ontological realization, not the identity of the entity itself. \\

        Persistence &
        The continued existence of an entity through a range of conditions or changes. &
        Persistence is a temporal or processual condition that raises questions about identity preservation but is not identical to identity. \\

        \hline
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}
