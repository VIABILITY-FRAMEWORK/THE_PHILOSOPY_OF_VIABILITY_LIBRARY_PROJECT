                                                Abstract
          We propose an axiomatic framework in which Information is not a primitive but the
      image of a constrained Abstraction Operator acting on partially accessible Identities. The
      primitives of the formalism are a substrate set Ω and a group G acting on it; Identity is
      the first derived object—an equivalence class in Ω/G—and Information is derived further
      downstream as the output of a structured approximation process.
          The framework rests on four axioms, contains one optimization principle (the Address
      Optimization Principle, with continuous routing costs), one working principle (the Cost–
      Quality Connection, stated as a conjecture to be proved in Paper II), and four immediate
      consequences that articulate its philosophical commitments. A fully worked example—fitting
      circles to finite point samples under SO(2)—instantiates every definition and verifies every
      condition.
          This paper is the first in a series that builds on the author’s previous formal developments
      on manifestation, representation, and spatial measure conservation. Its core commitment is:
      Reality is not made of information. Information is made from approximations of identities.


1     Introduction
The prevailing habit in several fields—physics, information theory, cognitive science—is to treat
“information” as a primitive, as in Wheeler’s “It from Bit” program. This paper explores an
alternative: the primitives are a substrate Ω and a group G acting on it. From these, Identity is
constructed as an equivalence class in Ω/G. Information is constructed later still, as the image
of an Abstraction Operator that approximates Identities from partial, transformation-dependent
Manifestations. The ontological order is:

        Ω, G −→ Identity −→ Manifestation −→ PseudoIdentity −→ Information.

1.1    Foundations
This paper builds on three prior developments by the author:

    • The Formal Theory of Manifestation (FTM) [1] establishes a categorical semantics
      for the distinction between invariant identity and contextual manifestation. Identities are
      objects in a base category, and manifestations are local sections of a sheaf over a context
      site. The central result is the Observer Incompleteness Theorem: a single observer is
      strictly insufficient to uniquely reconstruct an identity.
    • The General Theory of Representation (GTR) [2] proves that every deterministic
      representation factors canonically as Observable Restriction followed by Identification, and
      that these two mechanisms are the only sources of information loss in any representation
      system.

    • The Spatial Measure Conservation Principle (SMCP) [3] establishes that measure
      behaves as an invariant accounting quantity within any hierarchically closed reference
      domain, providing a variational foundation for reconstruction cost.

    The present paper provides the axiomatic foundation for how information—as approxima-

lationship between these works is discussed in Section 12.

1.2    Contribution of This Paper
Four axioms, one optimization principle with a nontrivial proof, one working principle (stated
as a conjecture for future proof), four immediate consequences, one cost function, one abstract
pseudometric, and a fully worked example. Every object has a domain. Every claim is either
proved or explicitly marked as an assumption, a definition, or a working principle. Section 12
relates each component of this framework to its counterparts in FTM, GTR, and SMCP.


2     Primitives and First Definitions
Definition 1 (Substrate and Group). The formalism has two primitives:

    • A set Ω (the “substrate”), whose elements are the most basic entities.

    • A group G acting on Ω from the left, written (g, ω) 7→ g · ω.

Definition 2 (Identity). An Identity is an equivalence class in the quotient space Ω/G. For
x ∈ Ω, its Identity is
                               Ix = {g · x : g ∈ G} ∈ Ω/G.                              (1)
We write I ∈ Ω/G to denote an arbitrary Identity.

Axiom 1 (Identity Approximation Principle — Epistemic Axiom). No observer has direct access
to an Identity I. Everything produced by or about I is strictly an approximation: P I ≈ I but
P I ̸= I. Any inexact approximation is a PseudoIdentity.

Status. This axiom is epistemic rather than mathematical. It specifies a limitation on observation—
no observer has direct access to an equivalence class itself, only to its representatives and their
transformations. It is not a consequence of the definition of Identity; it is an independent pos-
tulate about the relationship between observers and the objects they observe.

3     Abstraction Operators and Information
Definition 4 (Admissible Abstraction Operators). Let M be a set of Manifestations and PI
a set of PseudoIdentities. An Abstraction Operator is a function A : M → PI. The set A of
admissible Abstraction Operators consists of those satisfying three conditions:
    1. Consistency: If m ∈ MI , then A(m) is a PseudoIdentity of I, not of any I ′ ̸= I.
    2. H-Equivariance: A(h · m) = h · A(m) for all h ∈ H, whenever PI carries an H-action.
    3. Monotonicity (restricted): We restrict attention to the subclass of Abstraction Op-
       erators for which, when M1 ⊆ M2 , the reconstruction cost satisfies Crecon (A(M1 )) ≤
       Crecon (A(M2 )). This property is a working assumption introduced to permit the structural
       consequences that follow; it is not claimed to hold universally. For standard estimators
       such as least squares, adding i.i.d. samples reduces estimation variance at the cost of in-
       creased computation; the monotonicity condition is understood to hold for the estimation
       quality component, not necessarily for the total cost.
Axiom 2 (Abstraction). We define Information J to be the image of an admissible Abstraction
Operator:
                                   J = Im(A) ⊆ PI.                                      (3)


4     Membership
Axiom 3 (Holographic Membership Principle). Fix a network N of Identities. An Identity I
is a member of N iff there exists AI ∈ A such that AI (N ) ∈ PI is defined and non-empty. A
member must approximate the entire network, not merely its neighbors.

6     Abstraction Pseudometric and Drift
Definition 5 (Drift Pseudometric on PI). A drift pseudometric is a function δ : PI × PI →
R≥0 satisfying:

    1. δ(P I, P I) = 0 (reflexivity),

    2. δ(P Ii , P Ij ) = δ(P Ij , P Ii ) (symmetry),

    3. δ(P Ii , P Ik ) ≤ δ(P Ii , P Ij ) + δ(P Ij , P Ik ) (triangle inequality),

    4. If P Ii and P Ij are compatible (their restrictions to overlapping Manifestation data agree),
       then δ(P Ii , P Ij ) = 0.

We call this a pseudometric rather than a metric because δ(P Ii , P Ij ) = 0 does not imply P Ii =
P Ij : two distinct PseudoIdentities may be compatible on overlapping data and thus have zero
drift. This reflects the fact that distinct approximations can agree on all available evidence while
differing in their extrapolations.

   The drift pseudometric measures disagreement between two PseudoIdentities produced from
possibly different Manifestations of the same Identity. It does not require access to I itself.

Axiom 4 (IROP — Variational Principle). Among admissible Abstraction Operators A, the one
realized by a system minimizes (or approximately minimizes) Crecon .

11      Discussion
The framework establishes a coherent mathematical vocabulary organized around the ontological
priority of Identity over Information. The central inversion—Reality → Identity → Approxima-
tion → Information—distinguishes it from Wheeler, Shannon, and Floridi, while overlapping in
technical content with structural realism and gauge theory.

What this paper does not claim. It does not claim to explain consciousness, free will,
quantum mechanics, or any specific empirical phenomenon. It does not claim that the terms
“Identity” or “Information” as defined here correspond to their ordinary-language meanings. It
offers a formal language and one worked example; the question of whether this language is useful
for describing real systems is deferred to Papers II and III.

Open problems. (1) Prove the Cost–Quality Connection from the stated assumptions (Paper
II). (2) Derive IROP from more basic principles (see Appendix). (3) Characterize the class of
Abstraction Operators for which the Address Optimization Principle’s trade-off is non-trivial.
(4) Extend the framework to statistical manifolds with information-geometric structure.

12      Relationship to Previous Work
This paper is the first in a series that builds on three prior formal developments by the author.
This section summarizes the correspondences between the present framework and those earlier
works.

12.1     Formal Theory of Manifestation (FTM)
FTM [1] establishes a categorical semantics for the distinction between invariant identity and
contextual manifestation. The following elements of the present framework are structurally
similar to FTM constructs:

     • Identity as Ω/G (Definition 2): In FTM, identity is likewise constructed as an equiv-
       alence class under a group of allowable transformations [1, Definition 2.3].

     • Manifestation as H-orbit (Definition 4): This is analogous to FTM’s local sections of
       a sheaf over a context site [1, Definition 3.2]; the subgroup H plays the role of a context.

     • Identity Approximation Principle (Axiom 1): Can be related to the Observer In-
       completeness Theorem [1, Theorem 6.1]: a single observer is strictly insufficient to uniquely
       reconstruct an identity.

     • Membership (Axiom 3): Parallels the gluing condition for compatible local manifesta-
       tions [1, §4].

     • Drift Pseudometric (Definition 6): Plays a role similar to restriction maps ρU V :
       F(U ) → F(V ) that measure agreement on overlapping contexts.

     • Uniqueness of Identity (IC 1): Consistent with the Identity Preservation Theorem [1,
       Theorem 6.3], which establishes injectivity of the representation functor under separability
       conditions.

     • Incomplete Accessibility (IC 3): Follows from the same Observer Incompleteness
       Theorem.

12.2     General Theory of Representation (GTR)
GTR [2] proves that every deterministic representation factors canonically as Observable Re-
striction followed by Identification. The present framework contributes the axiomatic foundation
for how such representations are generated:

     • Abstraction Operator (Definition 5): The Consistency and Equivariance conditions
       are analogous to the Observable Restriction mechanism in GTR; the mapping into PI
       corresponds to the Identification step.

     • Information as Im(A) (Axiom 2): Information loss occurs through the choice of Ab-
       straction Operator—i.e., through which observables are retained (Restriction) and which
       distinguishable states are merged (Identification) [2, Principle 2.1].

     • Non-Uniqueness of Approximation (IC 4): Consistent with the GTR result that
       multiple representations can approximate the same underlying system.

12.3     Spatial Measure Conservation Principle (SMCP)
SMCP [3] establishes that measure behaves as an invariant accounting quantity within any
hierarchically closed reference domain:

     • Reconstruction Cost (Definition 7): Can be related to the cost function in SMCP [3,
       Definition 5.1].
     • IROP (Axiom 4): Inspired by the variational principle underlying the conservation of
       measure.
     • Cost–Quality Connection (WP 1): Parallels the Viability Monotonicity Theorem in
       FTM [1, Theorem 6.4]: more constrained policies have smaller viable state spaces.
     • Address Optimization Principle (Theorem 1): Structurally similar to the Minimum
       Observability Cover Theorem [1, Theorem 6.2], which concerns the minimum number of
       observers whose joint restriction map is injective.


13     Conclusion
We have presented an axiomatic framework whose primitives are a substrate Ω and a group
G, from which Identity, Manifestation, PseudoIdentity, and Information are successively con-
structed. The framework yields one optimization principle, one working principle, four immedi-
ate consequences, and a fully computable example. Its core commitment is:

 Reality is not made of information. Information is made from approximations of identities.

    This is Paper I of a projected series. It contains no interpretive applications; those belong
to the papers that follow.


A      On the Status of IROP
Three routes for deriving the variational principle (Axiom 4) from more basic postulates are
sketched:
    1. Least action / path-integral: Identify paths through PI and an action functional
       whose stationary-phase approximation yields IROP minimization. Blocked by the current
       lack of manifold structure on PI.
    2. Free-energy principle: Reinterpret Membership (Axiom 3) as a Markov blanket con-
       dition; derive IROP as the persistence condition for a stable PseudoIdentity distribution.
       Blocked by the absence of a probabilistic framework.
    3. Minimum description length: Show that Crecon -minimizing operators also minimize
       expected future reconstruction error under a suitable loss function. Blocked by the absence
       of a predictive error functional.
None of these routes is completed here.
