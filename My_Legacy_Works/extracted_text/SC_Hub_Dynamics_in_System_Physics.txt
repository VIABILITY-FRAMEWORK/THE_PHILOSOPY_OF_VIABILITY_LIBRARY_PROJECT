Hub Dynamics: Universal Conservation Laws for Flow-Mediation
                Nodes in Complex Systems
                                           Saeed Hasibi

                                         October 18, 2025


                                              Abstract
          We introduce Hub Dynamics (HD), a conservation-based mathematical framework that
      describes the behavior of hubs—generic flow-mediation nodes appearing across digital, eco-
      nomic, and biological systems. We prove a dimensional-invariant balance equation and
      derive a stochastic sedimentation model that predicts backlog accumulation under random
      disturbances. The framework provides universal capacity-margin rules for designing robust
      flow-mediation systems.


1     Introduction
Centralized mediation of flows is a recurrent motif of complex systems: cloud API gateways [1],
logistics hubs [2], and cellular membrane transporters [3] all buffer, convert, and forward pay-
loads. Despite domain-specific terminology, these nodes obey a common constraint: instanta-
neous output cannot permanently exceed input without an external energy source. No extant
theory unifies their behavior under one conservation principle.

2     Related Work
Flow conservation is classical in continuum mechanics [4] and traffic theory [5]. Queueing net-
works [6, 7] enforce local balance, yet require Poisson arrivals and exponential service. Supply-
chain dynamics focus on order-up-to policies [8] rather than physical conservation. Biochemical
flux balance analysis [9] assumes steady-state stoichiometry, ignoring transient backlogs. HD
generalizes these strands by treating any mediation node as a canonical hub irrespective of
arrival process or payload type.

3.3   Soundness Criterion
Theorem 2 (Soundness). A hub is sound iff

                                       E[vα+ ] ≥ E[vα− ],          ∀α.                           (4)

Proof. Suppose ∃α such that E[vα+ − vα− ] = −δ < 0. Then sα (t) has negative drift, hence
Pr(limt→∞ sα (t) = ∞) = 1, violating finite-capacity physical assumptions.

4     Potential Applications
4.1   Digital Hub: API Gateway
API gateways [1] mediate request-response flows where informational sediment accumulates as
queued requests. The balance equation describes how incoming request rates relate to processing
rates and queue lengths.

Cellular transporters [3] mediate molecular flows where biochemical sediment corresponds to
substrate accumulation. The stochastic model captures natural variability in biological systems.

5     Design Guidelines
From Theorem 2 we derive practical design rules:

    1. Capacity margin: Design hubs so that minα E[vα+ ]/E[vα− ] ≥ 1.2

    2. Sediment monitoring: Track sα (t) against theoretical distribution π(s)

    3. Degradation strategy: Implement flow shedding when sα (t) exceeds safe thresholds


6     Conclusion
Hub Dynamics provides a unified framework for analyzing flow-mediation nodes across diverse
domains. The conservation-based approach offers both theoretical insights and practical design
principles. Future work will focus on empirical validation and extensions to networked hub
systems.
