                                                 Abstract
         We develop a quantum framework in which the topology of space is ontologically prior to
     matter. Space is modeled as a growing directed acyclic graph (DAG), the scaffold, whose nodes
     are possible configurations and whose directed edges are possible transitions. Actualizing a
     transition consumes the corresponding edge and creates a permanent record. The record is the
     environment that makes alternative histories orthogonal in a consistent-histories formulation,
     and the resulting structure is intrinsically irreversible. Time is not a global parameter but
     the causal order of the actualized DAG. A Z2 -symmetric mirror scaffold supplies the dark
     sector; the ratio of mirror to baryonic mass is described by an effective power law whose
     slope β ≈ 0.785 is close to the value 3/4 implied by the baryonic Tully–Fisher relation for
     halos of constant mean overdensity. We test the framework on the SPARC catalogue of
     rotationally supported galaxies and on galaxy-cluster baryon fractions. The density-dependent
     pairing mechanism proposed in an earlier stage is excluded by SPARC at high significance:
     the measured residual slope of the radial acceleration relation against baryonic surface density
     is κ = +0.054 ± 0.023, whereas the mechanism predicts −0.3 to −0.4. The global mass law
     fits galactic rotation curves at the level of MOND with three global parameters, but a blind
     half-sample test shows the two models are statistically indistinguishable, and per-galaxy halo
     fits are vastly better in raw χ2 (with correspondingly more parameters). The mass law also
     over-predicts the dark-to-baryon ratio in clusters by a factor 1.2–4 and is therefore rejected as a
     universal law; it must be treated as a galaxy-scale phenomenological relation. The framework’s
     testable content reduces to the intrinsic-scatter bound, which we show is computable from the
     graph itself: the effective number of independent scaffold components inside a galactic radius
     equals the observability-cover number of the out-degree landscape of the actualized DAG,
     which is canonical and parameter-free. Preliminary simulation gives Neff ∼ 260 for multiplicity
     m = 1 and Neff ∼ 145 for m = 3 at N = 5000 nodes, both above the SPARC bound Neff ≥ 52
     that corresponds to a true intrinsic scatter of 0.06 dex. The framework therefore survives
     P4 on the graph side but loses P1 (cluster extrapolation) and P3 (density-dependence) as
     discriminators. We state six falsifiable predictions, report every test we have executed with its
     outcome, and list explicitly what is assumed rather than derived.

    1     Introduction
    1.1    The ontological inversion
    Contemporary physics treats spacetime as a continuous manifold on which quantum fields propagate,
    and in which matter curves geometry. This paper develops the opposite ordering. The fundamental
    object is the structure of connectivity among possible configurations; space, matter, and time are
    emergent. The position is closest in spirit to causal-set theory [1, 2], loop quantum gravity [3], and
    the Wolfram physics program [4], but the specific realization developed here uses consistent histories
    [5–7] combined with an explicit edge-consumption mechanism that supplies both irreversibility
    and decoherence.

    1.2    Core hypotheses
H1 [A] Scaffold. The universe is a growing DAG. Nodes are configurations; directed edges are possible
       transitions.

H2 [A] Selection with consumption. Selecting a transition consumes its edge and creates a
       permanent record. There is a single fundamental configuration; the language of “fronts” refers
       to epistemic decompositions of this configuration into locally connected parts (Remark 2.5).

H3 [A] Mirror duality. The scaffold has an exact Z2 partner. The realized mirror mass is related to
       the baryonic mass by the effective law of Section 5.3.

        Consequences. (i) The ordering of events is structural, in the sense that it is a consequence
    of H1–H2 rather than of entropy; the only temporal structure is the causal order on the actualized
    DAG (Section 4.1). (ii) Novelty is unbounded because the scaffold must grow. (iii) Selection among
    available transitions is probabilistic, with probabilities computed from the decoherence functional.

    1.3    Epistemic status
    Throughout the paper every statement carries a tag:
   [D] derived from the axioms;

    [A] axiom or definition;

    [P] phenomenological (physically motivated, derivation open);

    [F] fitted to data.
    Not claimed: a derivation of the Standard Model; a solution of the cosmological-constant problem;
    a derivation of the Einstein equations (open problem, Section 10.3); superiority over NFW or
    MOND in raw accuracy; and a universal mass law (this is rejected by the cluster test of Section 8).


    2     The Growing Scaffold
    2.1    Definition and axioms
    Definition 2.1 (Scaffold). A scaffold is a tuple S = (V, E, v0 , m, C) with: V a countable set of
    nodes (configurations); E ⊆ V × V a set of directed edges (possible transitions) with no directed
    cycle; v0 ∈ V an initial node; m : V → R≥0 a mass assignment; and C : V → R>0 a capacity
    assignment.
    Axiom 2.2 (Actualization). At step t the excitation sits at node vt . One edge (vt , u) ∈ E is
    selected from the currently available out-set of vt , actualized and consumed. The excitation moves
    to u, or terminates if no out-edge is available (the absorbing case of Section 3.2).

                                                      3
Axiom 2.3 (Consumption). A consumed edge is never reactivated, and it leaves a permanent
record in the edge-availability data of the scaffold.

Axiom 2.4 (Locality). Actualization follows only edges whose tail is the current node.

 Remark 2.5 (Epistemic fronts). There is one fundamental configuration. The word “fronts” refers
 to epistemic decompositions of the actualized structure into locally connected parts; fronts are
 not ontic entities that compete for edges. The single-configuration picture is the ontology of the
 framework.

 Remark 2.6 (Content of consumption). Because there is one excitation and the graph is acyclic,
 the excitation never revisits a node; consumption would be vacuous if the graph did not grow and
 no record were kept. The content of Axiom 2.3 is twofold.

(i) Record creation. Each consumed edge is a permanent record (Axiom 3.4), and this record is
    the environment that makes alternative histories orthogonal in Section 3. This is the primary
    content.

(ii) Growth constraint. The scaffold’s out-degrees that enter the attachment rule (Definition 2.8)
     are a function of the full history of actualized edges. The out-degree of a tail cannot decrease
     once an edge is consumed, so growth capacity is a real, history-dependent constraint, not a
     bookkeeping convention.

There is no competition between distinct consumers; the resource is the scaffold’s own growth
capacity.

3.4    Consumed edges as environment

4     Time and Irreversibility
4.1    Causal order
Definition 4.1 (Causal order). The actualized structure is a partially ordered set (Vact , ≺), where
u ≺ v iff there is a directed path in the actualized DAG from u to v. There is no global time
parameter. Two events are simultaneous iff they are incomparable in ≺. This is the only temporal
structure compatible with the single-configuration ontology (Remark 2.5).

                                                    5
4.2    Structural irreversibility
Proposition 4.2 (Irreversibility from record). Given Axioms 2.3 and 3.4, the reverse of any
non-trivial realized history γ has a consumed-edge record different from R(γ), so its class operator is
orthogonal to that of γ in D. The reverse history is therefore not a consistent alternative; structural
irreversibility follows.

Proof. The record R(γ) is a directed path; its reverse is a path in the reversed DAG with the
                 ←−−−                ←−−−
reversed record R(γ). Since R(γ) ̸= R(γ) for non-trivial γ, the L2 inner product vanishes in the
projective class-operator algebra and D(γ, γ rev ) = 0.

Remark 4.3 (Honest scope). The asymmetry follows from the axioms; it is not derived from more
primitive dynamics. The claim is that irreversibility is built into the scaffold’s definition, so no
low-entropy initial condition is needed to define a direction of time. We do not claim this resolves
the thermodynamic arrow, which must still emerge in the classical limit from coarse-graining.

4.3    Relation to causal sets
Proposition 4.4. Every realized history defines a causal set: the set Vact ordered by ≺. Conversely,
every causal set whose Hasse diagram is a directed tree arises from some history of the scaffold
with m = 1. With m > 1 the actualized structure is a DAG whose Hasse diagram is not a tree; the
resulting causal set is a general poset. The scaffold is thus a stochastic growth process on the space
of causal sets, related to the sequential-growth dynamics of Rideout and Sorkin [9, 10].

Conjecture 4.5. Does the process induced on the actualized causal set, after marginalizing over
the scaffold topology, belong to the covariant sequential-growth family of [9]? The edge-consumption
record is the most likely obstruction: birth probabilities depend on the full history of actualized
edges, and this history dependence may violate discrete general covariance. Checking this — (a)
constructively, by comparing the induced measure on causal sets to the Rideout–Sorkin family, and
(b) via the Bell-causality constraints of [9] — is the first technical task.

4.4    Initial condition
The initial state ρ0 is a boundary condition. A pure node state, a maximally mixed state, and a
thermal state are all admissible, and the framework does not select among them.


5     The Mirror Sector
5.1    Exact Z2 at the scaffold level
Definition 5.1 (Mirror scaffold). For every node v introduce a mirror node v̄, and for every edge
(u, v) a mirror edge (ū, v̄). The exchange v ↔ v̄ is an exact symmetry of the topology.

Remark 5.2. Symmetry of the topology does not imply symmetry of the realized matter state.
The two sectors may differ in initial conditions, reheating, and local pairing efficiency. This is
the standard route in mirror-matter cosmology and is what allows an exact Z2 to coexist with
cosmological constraints (Section 5.7).

5.2    The symmetric limit
Proposition 5.3. If every actualization in the main sector had a conjugate actualization in the
mirror sector with unit efficiency, the accumulated masses of the two sectors would be equal. The
observed cosmic ratio is not 1:1, so the realized pairing carries an overall sector-asymmetry factor.

5.5   Why the mirror sector is dark
The mirror sector is neutral under the Standard Model gauge group and does not couple to ordinary
photons. Kinetic mixing ε between the two U (1) factors [11] permits weak interactions, with values
near ε ∼ 10−9 discussed in the mirror-matter literature [12].

5.6   Halo structure
Mirror matter is dissipative: mirror baryons can cool and collapse. The standard mirror-matter
picture [12] maintains a hot, extended plasma halo supported by heating from ordinary supernova
energy transferred through kinetic mixing. We adopt this as an assumption and parametrize the
halo with a cored, truncated profile
                                                 ρc           1
                                 ρh (r) =               ·             .                           (9)
                                            1 + (r/rc )2 1 + (r/rt )2

This gives an enclosed mass growing linearly in r for rc ≪ r ≪ rt (flat rotation curve), has finite
total mass, and has an explicit parameter count: core radius rc , truncation radius rt tied to the
virial radius, and normalization fixed by the coupling law.

5.7   Cosmological consistency
A thermally equal mirror sector would add roughly 6 to the effective number of relativistic species,
far above the CMB/BBN limit. Viability requires a lower mirror temperature ratio x = T ′ /T after
reheating, with ∆Neff ∼ 6x4 and x ≲ 0.5 (asymmetric reheating; [13]).

6.1    Decoherence and the classical limit
Proposition 6.1 (Classical limit). Suppose histories that diverge at step k satisfy |D(γ, γ ′ )| ≲
exp[−Γ(T − k)] with Γ > 0 per step. Then as T → ∞ the decoherence functional becomes diagonal
and the dynamics reduces to a classical autoregressive process on the scaffold.
Proof sketch. For any two distinct histories γ ̸= γ ′ , let k be the first step at which they diverge. By
Axiom 3.4, the record sets R(γ) and R(γ ′ ) differ at step k, so the δ-factor in (5) forces D(γ, γ ′ ) = 0
up to a residual overlap of the pre-divergence parts. The residual is bounded by exp[−Γ(T − k)],
which vanishes as T → ∞. Hence D is diagonal in the long-time limit and the history sum reduces
to a classical probability distribution.

Proposition 6.7. The basins {B(h)}h∈H form a cover of Vact . Each basin is necessary: for

that hub is then indistinguishable from one that assigns the same value everywhere else. Hence no
proper subfamily is separating.

Remark 6.10 (Is r now fixed?). Yes, in the following precise sense: the free parameter r of

minimum separating subcover is a Set Cover problem rather than a counting problem. The number
of hubs is an upper bound on Neff . A full treatment of m > 1 is an open problem.

8.1    Prediction
The mass law (7), applied to a cluster with baryonic mass Mbar ∼ 1014 M⊙ , predicts

            Mmirror /Mbar = Λ (1014 /1010 )β−1 ≈ 84 · 104·(−0.215) ≈ 84 · 10−0.86 ≈ 11.6        (20)

8.2    Observation
Published X-ray and lensing baryon fractions (X-COP [24, 25], XXL [23], Gonzalez et al. [22]) give
dark-to-baryon ratios in the range 5.4–6.5 inside R500 , up to 12 only if lensing masses are biased
high by a factor of two. The prediction over-predicts by a factor of 1.2–4.

8.3    Consequence
The mass law is not a universal relation. A fit that forces saturation to the cosmic ratio requires
log M⋆ ∈ [10.5, 12.5] in the SPARC fit and worsens χ2 by ∆χ2 ≈ 5 × 103 . We therefore record the
following.

Remark 8.1 (P1 fails). The cluster extrapolation of the mass law is rejected at high significance.
The mass law (7) must be treated as a galaxy-scale phenomenological description, not a universal
one. It shares this status with the stellar-to-halo mass relations of standard galaxy formation,
which are also not extrapolable to clusters without additional physics.

    This is the framework’s most consequential negative result. It removes the cluster-scale
discriminative content of the model and confines the mass law to the galactic regime where it
cannot be distinguished from MOND or from standard halos.


9     Falsifiable Predictions
We list every prediction with its current status. Tested predictions have been evaluated against
published data; pending predictions require further work.

1. P1 (cluster extrapolation). Status: rejected. The predicted ratio 15.5–21.9 at Mbar ∼
   1014 M⊙ disagrees with the observed 5.4–6.5 (Section 8). The mass law is confined to galactic
   scales.

2. P2 (BTFR consistency). Status: consistent, non-discriminating. β = 1/2+d ln Rh /d ln Mbar
   must hold; with fixed-overdensity halos this gives β = 3/4. The fitted β = 0.785 satisfies it to
   within the systematic uncertainties. The condition is shared with standard halo models.

3. P3 (no surface-density dependence). Status: consistent, non-discriminating. κ = 0 is
   predicted by both the framework and MOND. The measured κ = +0.054 ± 0.023 is consistent
   with zero at ∼ 2σ.

4. P4 √ (intrinsic scatter). Status: pending, with resolution. The intrinsic scatter σv /v ≈
   1/(2 Neff ) is predicted, with Neff given by Theorem 6.9 as the number of out-degree landscape
   hubs inside the probed radius. Falsification: if a careful simulation of the growth rule for m = 1
   and m = 3 at N ∼ 5000 gives Neff < 14, the framework is falsified. Preliminary simulation
   gives Neff ∼ 260 (m = 1) and ∼ 145 (m = 3), both above the bound. A more careful simulation
   with full treatment of m > 1 is the immediate next step.

5. P5 (mirror cosmology). Status: pending. ∆Neff ∼ 6x4 with x ≲ 0.5. Measurements

10     Discussion
10.1   What has been achieved
1. A mathematically consistent selection formalism with non-vanishing class operators, an absorbing
   state that normalizes the history sum, and an explicit record-based decoherence structure
   (Section 3).
2. A single-configuration ontology in which the content of consumption is record creation and
   growth constraint, not competition between ontic fronts (Remarks 2.5–2.6).
3. A scaffold whose irreversibility is structural, with an honest account of its axiomatic origin
   (Proposition 4.2).
4. A well-posed steady state (Theorem 6.4) and a graph-canonical choice of restart distribution
   (Definition 6.6, Theorem 6.9).
5. A resolution of the earlier underdetermination of Neff : the effective number of components is
   the number of out-degree hubs, a graph-canonical quantity, and preliminary simulation gives
   Neff ∼ 145–260, above the SPARC bound (Section 6.6).
6. A test protocol that has been executed once on SPARC and on clusters, with the following
   outcomes: the density-dependent pairing mechanism is excluded; the mass law fits galactic
   rotation curves at MOND level with three global parameters; the cluster extrapolation of the
   mass law is rejected (Sections 7.4–8).

10.2   Limitations
1. Galactic data do not discriminate. The framework fits at the level of MOND and is
   indistinguishable from it out of sample.
2. Raw fit quality is poor. χ2 /N ≈ 7.5 under the stated error model; the framework’s statistical
   advantage over halo models is parameter economy, not accuracy.
3. The mass law is descriptive, not derivable. Its amplitude (∼ 84) is unexplained and its
   cluster extrapolation fails.
4. Neff resolution is partial. Theorem 6.9 fixes r via the out-degree landscape for m = 1; the
   m > 1 case requires a Set Cover computation and is an upper bound.
5. Dissipative halos. A stable extended mirror halo requires continued heating through kinetic
   mixing; its dynamics, including the Bullet Cluster and cluster lensing, must be examined
   quantitatively.
6. Foundations. The reduction to sequential growth (Conjecture 4.5), the continuum limit, the
   Einstein equations, and the thermodynamic arrow are not established.
7. Systematics. Stellar mass-to-light ratios, distances, inclinations, the error floor, and the
   aperture definition were not marginalized in the exploratory SPARC run.

                                                13
10.3     Open problems
1. Explain the mass law and its amplitude from the scaffold. Any pairing mechanism must be
   density-independent.
2. Extend Theorem 6.9 to m > 1 via the Set Cover formulation; compute Neff for multiple
   realizations and check the Neff ≥ 52 bound robustly.
3. Settle Conjecture 4.5: does the induced process on the actualized causal set belong to the
   covariant sequential-growth family?
4. Connect the growing scaffold to FRW expansion and recover the Einstein equations in the
   continuum limit.
5. Pre-register the SPARC protocol and re-run with mass-to-light ratios, distances, and inclinations
   marginalized.


11      Conclusion
We have developed a framework in which the topology of space is prior to matter, formalized as a
growing DAG with an edge-consumption mechanism. The framework’s formal content is:
 • a consistent-histories formulation with non-vanishing class operators, a normalized history sum,
   and a record-based decoherence structure (Sections 3–4);
 • structural irreversibility from record orthogonality (Proposition 4.2);
 • a PageRank-type steady state with a canonical, graph-determined restart distribution via the
   out-degree landscape (Theorem 6.4, Definition 6.6, Theorem 6.9).
     The framework’s empirical content, after the SPARC and cluster tests reported here, is:
 • a density-dependent pairing mechanism excluded at high significance (κ = +0.054 ± 0.023);
 • a global mass law that fits galactic rotation curves at MOND level with three global parameters
   and is statistically indistinguishable from MOND out of sample;
 • a mass law that over-predicts cluster dark-to-baryon ratios by a factor of 1.2–4 and is therefore
   rejected as a universal law;
 • a graph-canonical Neff whose preliminary value (∼ 145–260) satisfies the SPARC bound
   Neff ≥ 52 and therefore keeps P4 as a live falsifiable prediction.
    On galactic scales the framework is not distinguished from MOND or from standard halo
models with a stellar-to-halo relation. Its testable content is now the Neff prediction (P4), the
mirror-sector cosmology (P5–P6), and the mathematical open problems of Conjecture 4.5 and
the m > 1 extension of Theorem 6.9. If P4 fails on a more careful simulation, the framework’s
empirical content reduces to the excluded mechanism of Section 7.4 and the negative cluster result
of Section 8; if P5–P6 are confirmed, the mirror sector retains a cosmological foothold. We have
made every step of this assessment explicit and have not claimed more than the data support.


A      Sketch of the classical limit
For histories γ, γ ′ that first diverge at step k, Axiom 3.4 makes the interference term proportional
to the overlap of their edge-availability records. If each further step suppresses that overlap by a
factor exp(−Γ) with Γ > 0, then |D(γ, γ ′ )| is bounded by exp[−Γ(T − k)], which vanishes as T
grows, so D becomes diagonal. The rate Γ is the phenomenological input of Remark 3.5; deriving
it from the scaffold’s structure is an open problem.

B     Out-degree landscape: additional detail
The discrete flow step (13) has a unique image for each node except when there are ties. In the
tie case, choose the child with smaller index (or smaller position) as tiebreaker; the resulting
partition is independent of the tiebreaker up to a measure-zero set of configurations. With the
tiebreaker, iteration of F reaches a local maximum in finitely many steps (since L is integer-valued
and strictly increases at each step, and Vact is finite). This proves existence and uniqueness of the
basin decomposition.
